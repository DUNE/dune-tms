1Passages and resegmentation

One fixed muon crossing of one bar (plane 28, bar 28), cut by hand into 1, 2, 5, 10 or 20 Geant4 steps. The physics is identical in every case, so any change with the step count is an artifact of the bookkeeping. 5000 throws per point (ArtificialResegmentationTest).

4Channel readout

How one electronics channel turns light into hits. Two muons cross the same bar, the second one a time Δt after the first, and we count the hits the channel reports: 1 means the two crossings were combined into one hit (or the second was lost), 2 means they were read out separately. Δt is scanned from 0 to 1000 ns in 10 ns steps, 100 throws per step (DetSimStageScan pair).

What the channel does after the first crossing (t = 0)

  • Readout window, 0–120 ns. Light arriving in the window is added to the hit that opened it. A second crossing here gives no new hit: 1 hit.
  • Deadtime, 120–620 ns (only when configured; off by default). The channel is busy and a crossing is lost: still 1 hit.
  • Zombie time, the last 100 ns of the deadtime (only when configured). A crossing there is not lost; it is read out as a new hit when the channel comes back at 620 ns: 2 hits.
  • After that the channel is free and the second crossing opens its own window: 2 hits.

Default readout: legacy and new

5Timing-mode front end (option)

With FrontEndTimingMode = true, each channel's photons are summed through the Citiroc-1A fast shaper (15 ns peaking) and a discriminator gives the hit time and time over threshold (ToT) in 0.5 ns TDC steps. Scanned over the discriminator threshold; 2000 throws per point.

6Truth provenance

The new pipeline tags each photon with the particle that produced it, so a hit can be credited to the particle that made most of its light as well as the one that deposited most of its energy. Real spills, hits above threshold with more than one contributing particle.
